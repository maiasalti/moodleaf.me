# Moodleaf — Mission Statement

## Core Mission

**Moodleaf helps readers find books that match how they actually feel, not just what's trending.**

The reading experience is deeply personal — it depends on your mood, your energy, what you're craving emotionally. Existing recommendation tools (Amazon, Goodreads, BookTok) optimize for popularity, social proof, and purchase conversion. They answer "what's popular" but not "what's right for me *right now*."

Moodleaf flips this. Instead of algorithms watching your behavior, *you* tell us what you want through intuitive sliders. No sign-up required. No tracking. Just honest, transparent matching between your mood and the books in our library.

## Core Values

1. **Mood-first discovery** — The sliders are the product. They let you articulate preferences you might not have words for ("I want something slow but not boring, dark but not hopeless").

2. **Transparency** — Every match percentage is explainable. You can see *why* a book scored the way it did. No black-box algorithms.

3. **No gatekeeping** — The full experience works without an account. Signing up is a bonus, not a barrier.

4. **Taste over trends** — We don't surface books because they're bestsellers. A 94% match for a lesser-known literary novel ranks above a 70% match for a viral BookTok hit.

5. **Warm, human, editorial** — Moodleaf feels like a friend's bookshelf, not a tech product. The design is organic, the palette is warm, the typography is bookish.

## What Moodleaf is NOT

- Not a social network (no feeds, no followers, no likes)
- Not a marketplace (no affiliate links, no purchase pressure)
- Not a reading tracker (Goodreads already does that)
- Not an AI chatbot ("tell me what to read") — sliders give users agency

## Who it's for

Readers who:
- Know what they *feel* like reading but can't describe it in a search box
- Are tired of seeing the same 20 books recommended everywhere
- Want to explore beyond their usual genres
- Trust their own taste more than algorithms

## Where it's going

Moodleaf starts with books. The slider-based discovery model can extend to film, TV, music, podcasts — any media where *mood* matters more than *genre*. The vision is to become the go-to tool for mood-based media discovery.
